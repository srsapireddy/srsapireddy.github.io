<div class="about-section">
  <h3>Overview</h3>
  <p>
    The <strong>Intelligent Neural &amp; Signal Systems Laboratory (INSys Lab)</strong> is a faculty-led research group focused on the development of
    efficient, interpretable, and hardware-aware methods for intelligent signal processing and edge computing systems.
    The lab explores how principles from signal processing, statistical learning, and neural systems can be integrated to enable real-time intelligence
    under constraints on power, latency, and memory.
  </p>
  <p>
    INSys Lab adopts a systems-level research perspective that spans algorithm design, evaluation methodology, and deployment-aware considerations.
    The lab’s research emphasizes approaches that are not only accurate, but also practical for embedded, edge, and cyber-physical platforms.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="about-section">
  <h3>Research Focus</h3>
  <p>Research activities in INSys Lab are organized around the following themes:</p>
  <ul>
    <li>Intelligent RF signal analysis and modulation classification</li>
    <li>Lightweight and hardware-aware neural and statistical learning models</li>
    <li>Signal-domain feature extraction and interpretable learning frameworks</li>
    <li>Edge intelligence for embedded and resource-constrained systems</li>
    <li>Robust and trustworthy intelligent signal processing architectures</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div class="about-section">
  <h3>Research Philosophy and Methodology</h3>
  <p>
    INSys Lab emphasizes principled and deployment-conscious research methodologies. Rather than relying exclusively on large or opaque models,
    the lab prioritizes approaches that balance performance, efficiency, and interpretability. Core methodological principles include:
  </p>
  <ul>
    <li>Integrating signal processing insights with learning-based models</li>
    <li>Designing algorithms with explicit consideration of hardware constraints</li>
    <li>Evaluating methods using accuracy, runtime, memory, and power metrics</li>
    <li>Bridging theoretical foundations with system-level validation</li>
  </ul>
  <p>
    This philosophy aligns with the lab’s goal of producing research outcomes that translate effectively from theory to practice.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="about-section">
  <h3>Tools and Platforms</h3>
  <p>
    Research in INSys Lab leverages a combination of simulation, software prototyping, and hardware-oriented evaluation tools, including:
  </p>
  <ul>
    <li>Python and MATLAB for signal analysis and algorithm development</li>
    <li>RF signal simulation and statistical feature extraction pipelines</li>
    <li>Embedded and edge AI development environments</li>
    <li>Hardware-aware evaluation workflows for runtime and resource analysis</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div class="about-section">
  <h3>Student Involvement and Mentoring</h3>
  <p>
    Student engagement is a central component of INSys Lab. The lab provides hands-on research opportunities for undergraduate and graduate students through:
  </p>
  <ul>
    <li>Independent study and directed research projects</li>
    <li>Senior design and capstone project supervision</li>
    <li>Exploratory research roles in signal processing and edge intelligence</li>
  </ul>
  <p>
    Students gain experience in problem formulation, algorithm development, experimental evaluation, and technical communication.
    Mentoring emphasizes both foundational understanding and practical research skills applicable to academia and industry.
  </p>
  <p>
    Details of current and previously mentored students are available on the
